System architecture · research implementation

From Agent Decisions
to Cross-Round Market Feedback

agent-network is a single-product supply-chain market simulation. Sellers and Factories form procurement intentions through structured decisions and finite-stage negotiation; the market engine executes capacity allocation, transportation, warehousing, consumption, and economic accounting, producing traceable state changes across multiple rounds.

01 / Overview

What is the overall system made of?

The system separates an Agent’s economic judgment, market protocol, resource execution, and research observability into distinct responsibilities. The LLM can form strategy, quantity, and price judgments; all authoritative cash, inventory, capacity, and order states are maintained by the program.

Figure 1. Schematic of responsibility relationships. Arrows indicate information or execution dependencies; they do not imply that all calls run in parallel, nor that Factory B is enabled in the formal D0 run.

Modeling target

A simplified standard packaging-tape supply chain: consumers, sellers, factories, logistics, and warehousing. Finite supply capacity and cross-round delivery connect current procurement to future inventory.

Research target

How strategy selection, replenishment scale, bilateral negotiation, and supplier networks co-evolve with cash, inventory, and delivery timing. The system preserves single-run trajectories and supports later replication and mechanism testing.

This page is based on the current workspace source code, version contracts, and the formal 60R artifacts. The latest committed code date remains 2026-08-20; the document-generation date does not imply a new simulation-version upgrade.

An Agent identity is not the same thing as a single LLM request. In one round, a Seller may separately call strategy, quantity, RP, and negotiation interfaces; these requests share the same actor identity but have independent decision IDs, observations, schemas, and records.

Consumer v1 and v1.1

The project retains a v1 compatibility path. There is no consumer LLM, multi-product substitution, credit, or connection to a real external market.

03 / Economic clock

Execution order within one market round

Advance existing supply orders

Check production completion, pickup, and delivery. Delivery converts Seller in-transit inventory into on-hand inventory and updates resource and ETA records.

Consumer choice and sales

Read demand events for the current round and form purchase or no-purchase outcomes. Successful sales reduce inventory and increase cash; zero-inventory rounds are then recorded.

Seller strategy and procurement decisions

First update the high-level strategy, then form quantity and RFQ routing decisions; RP and negotiation inputs are constructed from the actor’s currently visible state and history.

Valuation, negotiation, and contract clearing

The Factory forms a valuation and both sides interact under a finite protocol. Agreed tentative contracts then pass cash, capacity, logistics, and warehousing checks.

Confirm orders and record economics

Commit valid orders, reserve resources, and perform the corresponding cash handling; record Factory operating costs. Production is typically scheduled for later rounds.

Generate metrics and end-of-round snapshots

Record market metrics, Seller trajectories, accounting state, and strategy outcomes. The online health gate determines whether the simulation may proceed to the next round.

Consumption in a round occurs before procurement decisions in that same round. End-of-round inventory, observations at decision time, and the previous round’s end state therefore have different temporal meanings. The formal exports preserve these definitions, and lag analysis should align stages before computing relationships.

The full cross-round feedback path is: consumption and sales → Seller replenishment → Factory scheduling and backlog → production/transport/delivery → Seller inventory and stockout state → subsequent strategy and procurement. This execution dependency explains how the mechanisms connect; a single trajectory alone still cannot identify the causal effect of each link.

04 / Seller architecture

Seller uses a staged decision architecture

High-level strategy, replenishment quantity, willingness to pay, and negotiation actions each have separate interfaces. Strategy provides an operating direction; later stages combine state information to make concrete decisions subject to hard market constraints.

① Seven-strategy scoring and selection

ScoringMetaController requires the model to score all seven strategies, provide a ranking, select the top-ranked strategy, and return strength, tolerance, and a rationale. The validator checks fields, score ranges, ranking consistency, and selected_strategy; the final selection comes from the model’s valid ranking, and the program does not independently recompute an “optimal strategy.”

StrategyOperating orientationMain tendencies exposed by the Adapter
RELIABILITY_PRIORITYPrioritize reliable supply and deliveryIncrease the weight on ETA reliability and acceptance tendency
LIQUIDITY_PRIORITYPreserve cash and reduce capital tied up in replenishmentEmphasize price; reduce replenishment and bid aggressiveness; increase COUNTER tendency
MARGIN_PRIORITYControl procurement price and protect gross marginEmphasize price, reduce bid aggressiveness, and increase COUNTER tendency
GROWTH_PRIORITYExpand sales and supply capacityIncrease replenishment, bidding, and acceptance tendencies
DIVERSIFICATIONReduce dependence on individual suppliersIncrease ranking weight for diversified procurement
BUFFERINGMaintain an inventory bufferIncrease replenishment tendency with moderately more aggressive bidding
WAIT_AND_SEEDelay commitment and continue observingReduce replenishment and bid aggressiveness and increase the tendency to wait

The Adapter’s effective strength is typically strength × (1 − secondary parameter), producing preference metadata. It is not an independent learning model and does not directly authorize illegal actions or rewrite cash and orders.

② Coverage-aware quantity autonomy

CoverageAwareQuantityController shows the model expected demand, on-hand/in-transit supply, cash, the selected strategy, and compliant history. The model returns BUY/WAIT, target coverage in rounds, and a non-negative integer quantity. Inventory-to-demand coverage is a descriptive scale; the current interface has no fixed inventory target, EOQ, or automatically recommended quantity. WAIT must correspond to quantity 0, and format-repair retries cannot change the original quantity decision.

③ Supplier routing and Seller RP

Supplier-candidate ranking is wired into the RFQ through action wiring. Seller RP is formed in a specific Seller–Factory–quantity–round context and records the raw reservation price, the cash-affordability cap, and the effective RP. The formal engine also combines an existing buyer economic boundary to limit the maximum acceptable price entering the protocol.

RP_effective_per_unit = min(LLM_RP_per_unit, Seller_cash / quantity)
Buyer protocol upper bound = min(existing buyer boundary, RP_effective_per_unit × quantity)

④ Negotiation and retail pricing

The Seller negotiation LLM receives the active offer, legal actions, and private operating information for the current session and returns a structured action. Retail prices in the current market engine are still adjusted by explicit rules, for example based on inventory coverage and changes in the market median price; high-level strategy selection itself does not directly output a retail price.

Strategy-position control and remaining issues

The presentation list is deterministically permuted by a hash of seed, Seller, round, and decision epoch; economic state is not part of the permutation key. Historical diagnostics found that selection was sensitive to ordering, so the list order is now controlled. However, transmitted JSON uses sort_keys=True, and some strategy mappings are still alphabetically ordered. This does not justify claiming that position bias has been fully eliminated. In the formal 60R run, BUFFERING accounts for 61% and LIQUIDITY_PRIORITY for 18%; these are observed selection frequencies.

05 / Factory architecture

Factory: hard cost floor, subjective valuation, and negotiation are separated

Factory pricing has three distinct concepts: a program-computed hard cost floor, an LLM-generated reservation price, and an opening ask. The current validation relationship is:

opening_ask_total ≥ reservation_price_total ≥ hard_floor_total

In D0, Architecture A forms one Factory valuation before negotiation begins and then freezes the reservation price for that session. Formal valuation inputs include base unit cost, backlog, per-round capacity, reserved capacity, next-round available capacity, and expected production-completion round. Runtime support for a field does not mean that complete operating history is passed on every call; the current production policy passes valuation negotiation history as None.

Architecture A Enabled in D0

The program automatically computes and provides authoritative fact/consequence packages, such as marginal profit for a given offer, current load, and scheduling consequences. The Factory LLM uses these facts to make negotiation judgments.

Computed facts are not recommended prices or recommended actions; legality and market execution remain the responsibility of the protocol and clearing.

Architecture B Evaluated in a controlled comparison

The Factory receives raw legal state and deterministic tool interfaces and may autonomously call a calculator or production preview; the comparison contract limits it to at most three tool calls.

This is an alternative decision interface that has been designed and tested. The formal D0 runner uses Architecture A.

Factory operating economics has two additional layers: the shadow layer computes cost projections such as LOW/BASE/HIGH, while the active layer actually records material cash obligations and operating costs under the selected mode. D0 is configured as MATERIAL_PLUS_OPERATING / BASE. Factory observation, valuation, protocol floor, and accounting have separate responsibilities.

06 / Protocol & fulfillment

What mechanisms connect negotiation to a real order?

Five-stage finite negotiation

StageActorProtocol meaning
1FactorySubmit the opening ask
2SellerRespond to the current offer
3FactoryRespond; allowed delivery-option changes are handled under protocol constraints
4SellerFinal Seller response/proposal opportunity
5FactoryFinal ACCEPT or REJECT; no further COUNTER is allowed

Quantity is fixed by the RFQ, and price proposals use an explicit complete offer bundle. ACCEPT must reference the currently valid offer; the protocol has no midpoint settlement or automatic trade when prices cross. Boundaries, legal actions, minimum substantive concessions, and stage progression are controlled by a unified state machine, and Rule and LLM policies share the same protocol.

Tentative contract → clearing → atomic commit

Agreement in negotiation only creates a TentativeContract; cash, inventory, and resources have not yet moved. A_SYNC clearing waits for the valid results in the batch, applies a stable priority order and tie handling, and then attempts joint resource allocation.

  1. Check the cash lower bound and, starting from the next round, search for a cross-round Factory production allocation.
  2. Find a contiguous logistics-capacity window that begins no earlier than production completion.
  3. Check whether occupied warehouse capacity, all undelivered reservations, and the candidate order can jointly fit.
  4. Verify the full cash outlay including Factory, logistics, and warehousing charges.
  5. If every check passes, commit resource reservations, the order, and economic changes; on failure, restore related state using the transactional undo journal.

Warehouse capacity uses a conservative constraint and does not pre-assume how much space future sales may release. A SupplyOrder stores production allocations, pickup round, delivery round, transport duration, resource identities, fees, and tentative/confirmed ETA differences. The execution service advances the order in subsequent rounds using this information.

Order confirmation immediately increases the Seller’s “purchased but not yet delivered” in-transit quantity; delivery converts it to on-hand inventory. Factory backlog is the committed quantity not yet produced. It is part of supply-fulfillment state and must not be added again to Seller total inventory.

07 / Accounting & economics

Cash, inventory assets, and profit use different accounting definitions

FinanceService is responsible for changes in cash and profit state; InventoryLedger tracks initial inventory, purchased in-transit inventory, delivery, and FIFO cost of goods sold by lot. A confirmed purchase causes a cash outflow while the goods enter the inventory ledger as an in-transit asset; consumer sales increase cash and consume inventory.

The Seller shadow model uses a default holding-cost rate of 1% per round and an in-transit capital rate of 0.5% per round, calculated on the corresponding end-of-round inventory values; stockout opportunity loss is attributed only to cancelled orders with an explicit Seller association. These costs are not automatically deducted from Seller cash. Current formal strategy/quantity observations use cash-like profit, while the RP shadow_economics field is empty. Therefore, “shadow metrics are computed” must not be rewritten as “all LLMs make decisions using shadow profit.”

Factory active economics produces actual material cash flows and operating expenses. Cash registration of material obligations avoids double-deducting material cost already recognized in transaction gross profit; operating costs have independent provenance and idempotent registration keys. Factory active costs and Seller shadow deductions are therefore different treatments.

08 / Observations & memory

An Agent can read only the information it is authorized to know

Seller-visible observations

Its own cash, inventory, in-transit inventory, recent sales, demand expectation, procurement history, and current strategy; offers addressed to itself; and, when allowed by configuration, its own realized ETA history.

Factory-private observations

Its own costs and capacity, backlog, reservation schedule, current-session offers, and deterministic factual consequences; private floors, internal pressure, and data from other sessions are not disclosed to the Seller.

Structured history

StrategyHistory and MarketHistoryIndex connect prior selections, orders, and realized outcomes. Each history segment has a time window, actor ownership, and provenance.

Frozen inputs and causal timing

Observations enter the decision context as immutable/normalized records; ownership, field allowlists, hashes, and temporal conditions limit cross-actor leakage and prevent future outcomes from entering earlier decisions.

“Memory” here mainly means traceable historical state and outcome projections. The system does not train model weights in each round, and strategy switching cannot by itself be described as reinforcement learning.

The ETA module has a unified ledger, stage events, lifecycle tracking, and private historical views constructed by Seller–Factory pair. Formal D0 connects Seller own realized history to strategy observations; however, the current RP production builder does not populate current ETA or own ETA history, so ETA visibility is not identical across decision interfaces.

09 / Runtime & reproducibility

Online decisions, concurrency, failure handling, and replay

Two production call paths

Strategy, quantity, and Seller RP use ProductionJSONDecisionTransport, which stores requests, raw responses, fingerprints, model identity, and private audit files. Factory valuation and bilateral negotiation use AsyncNegotiationStageService with private negotiation auditing; the formal concurrency configuration is 3.

Each active negotiation stage uses an A_SYNC barrier and advances only after all results for that stage have been collected. The shared concurrency context tracks requests using agent ID and snapshot ID and provides same-actor serialization, stale-snapshot checks, and duplicate-write checks. Strategy, quantity, and RP use synchronous bridges in the formal runner; max_concurrency=3 must not be interpreted as three-way concurrency across the entire decision chain.

Failure semantics and runtime health

Missing global configuration or failure of a critical decision aborts the corresponding path; negotiation schema/protocol failures are recorded as session failures, but not every schema rejection is guaranteed to stop the entire market. The online health gate stops subsequent rounds when usable rate is below 80% for two consecutive rounds.

The formal Provider path supports PRIMARY → FALLBACK: authentication/quota failures can trigger a switch, and fallback is latched by credential scope. HTTP 402 is classified as API_QUOTA_FAILURE. The formal runner still requires PRIMARY/FALLBACK environment variables; this describes code behavior and does not imply that current account balance, keys, or online service status have been checked.

Replay and random sources

Recorded replay loads saved responses by request fingerprint, skips online model calls, and then compares semantic projections of market outcomes. The latest round-hash fix excludes transport-provenance differences such as LIVE_LLM/RECORDED_REPLAY while preserving market state and decision outcomes.

10 / Engineering boundaries

What engineering boundaries were created by the R1–R8 refactor?

The system retains SimulationEngine as the round-orchestration entry point while progressively separating construction, observation, finance, state mutation, commit, and recording into explicit components. The Engine still contains multiple compatibility entry points and historical reference implementations, so it should not be described as a fully minimized pure orchestrator.

The storage layer supports SQLite and JSONL, while the analysis layer handles market metrics, profit projections, concentration, ETA, and anomaly observations.

Historical validation and remaining engineering debt

The R1–R8 closeout reports record staged behavioral-consistency gates; the historical R8 report shows 576 passed / 16 xfailed and comparisons across nine scenario classes. These are validation records from that time; this documentation task did not rerun the full test suite.

Recorded engineering debt includes legacy reference code in the Engine, some full-history scans, duplicated ETA projections, and end-of-run data that are still materialized centrally. Anomaly detection/SVD is optional shadow analysis and should not be described as a module that already drives Agent behavior in D0.

11 / Evolution

From the base market to the current version

Base market and Consumer

Established multi-role actors, demand plans, inventory/cash state, consumer fulfillment, and JSONL/SQLite records; developed the v1.1 consumer-choice model and A_SYNC decision semantics.

P1 · Joint physical scheduling

Jointly checked cross-round Factory production, contiguous logistics windows, and warehouse capacity; introduced tentative/confirmed ETA, stable conflict handling, and atomic candidate commits.

P2 · Boundaries, protocol, clearing, and replay

Clarified terminology and economic boundaries; developed Factory pressure observations, the ETA ledger, and five-stage finite negotiation, then connected Seller/Factory LLM policies and full market recorded replay.

P3.0–P3.2 · Information and economic treatment

P3.0 added Seller-owned ETA information interfaces; P3.1 established read-only shadow economics; P3.2 moved from Factory shadow operating-cost projections to active material/operating-cost accounting.

P3.3 · Strategy, action wiring, and price formation

The seven-strategy library, Adapter, and historical-outcome wiring were progressively developed; experiments covered strategy responses, RP, bilateral Factory judgment, and A/B decision interfaces. The R1–R8 refactor clarified engineering responsibilities during this evolution.

P3.4 · Long-horizon production decision path

Connected concurrent decision context, production transport, Seller strategy/quantity/RP, Architecture A Factory valuation, and negotiation; added scoring semantics, presentation order, strict history, coverage-based quantity logic, and Supplier-routing checks.

P3.4 / D0 · Current frozen baseline

Fixed the Factory cross-event-loop runtime issue, improved online health gating, Provider failure handling, and failover classification, and finally corrected transport-provenance differences in the round hash before preserving the formal 60R run.

14 / Paper-ready description

Wording suitable for a paper’s Methods section

We constructed a discrete-round multi-agent market simulation for a single-product supply chain. The system contains Sellers, Factories, logistics, warehouses, and programmatic consumers. Sellers use staged language-model interfaces to form operating strategies, replenishment quantities, reservation prices, and negotiation actions; Factories form valuations from private cost and capacity information and participate in bilateral bargaining under a finite-stage protocol. The market engine performs legality checks, contract clearing, cross-round production and logistics scheduling, and authoritative updates of cash, inventory, and order states. The system uses actor-scoped observations and historical projections and records decision provenance, economic ledgers, and market trajectories to support auditing and recorded replay. The formal P3.4/D0 implementation uses Factory Architecture A with precomputed factual consequences and completed one fixed-seed 60-round run; the resulting trajectory is used to describe temporal changes in variables such as strategy switching, transaction networks, and inventory, with conclusions restricted to descriptive associations from a single trajectory.
